# **135.71% Above Pre-Pandemic Levels: A Data-Led eSIM Market Expansion Strategy**

## **Context**

GlobalComm was allocating eSIM acquisition spend based on inbound arrival volume, creating a risk of over-investing in saturated low-margin markets while missing higher-value travel corridors. The objective was to identify destinations with stronger revenue potential using tourism demand, spending, and recovery trends.

## **Approach**

* Cleaned and merged multi-source UN Tourism datasets using Python (Pandas)
* Standardized arrivals, expenditure, transport, accommodation, and trip-purpose metrics
* Built derived KPIs: expenditure per arrival, value premium, volatility (CV), recovery growth
* Applied correlation analysis, Pareto concentration analysis, and trend modeling across 2019–2024

## **Key Findings**

* **Demand is highly concentrated:** Top 20% of markets generated **76.3% of global arrivals**, indicating heavy competition in major destinations.
* **Revenue growth outpaced visitor growth in select markets:** El Salvador (**+135.7% spend vs +49.9% arrivals**) and Saudi Arabia (**+133.5% spend vs +65.0% arrivals**) led recovery efficiency.
* **High air-arrival markets create longer usage windows:** Dominican Republic (**8.0 days**) and Saudi Arabia (**7.7 days**) had the longest average stays among major air markets.
* **Business-heavy markets spend more:** Canada (**39.8% business share**) and Belgium (**31.0%**) recorded above-average expenditure per traveler.
* **Primary markets vary in stability:** Saudi Arabia (**CV 76.0%**) and UK (**61.1%**) were highly volatile, while Hong Kong SAR (**18.9%**) and Poland (**20.6%**) were more stable.

## **Recommendations**

* Reallocate budget toward markets where spending growth exceeds arrival growth
* Prioritize long-stay air markets for higher data usage and top-up potential
* Target business-heavy corridors with premium bundle offers
* Balance growth markets with stable markets to improve acquisition efficiency

## **Tools**

Python, Pandas, NumPy, Matplotlib, Seaborn
